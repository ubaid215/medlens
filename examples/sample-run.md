🩺 Cardiac Pain — Explain

*[image 1: 3D heart with fine yellow nerve fibres running to the sympathetic trunk beside the
upper thoracic spine. Labels: "Myocardium", "Cardiac branches", "Sympathetic trunk", "T1–T4"]*

1. The heart's pain nerves
- Pain fibres leave the heart in the cardiac branches of the sympathetic trunk.
- They enter the spinal cord through the posterior roots of T1–T4.

*[image 2: spinal cord cross-section at T2 showing a heart fibre and a skin fibre meeting at
the same dorsal horn neuron. Labels: "Heart fibre", "Skin fibre", "Posterior root", "Dorsal horn"]*

2. Why the pain feels like skin pain
- Heart and skin nerves meet on the same spinal neurons.
- The brain reads the signal as coming from the skin of T1–T4.
💡 Exam tip: this is "convergence" — the basis of all referred pain.

*[image 3: body map, anterior view, red shading on upper chest, inner left upper arm, neck
and jaw. Labels: "Upper chest", "Medial arm (T2)", "Neck", "Jaw"]*

3. Where the patient feels it
- Upper chest and inner arm (intercostobrachial nerve, T2).
- Sometimes neck and jaw, from spread inside the spinal cord.
- Can be crushing pain or only mild discomfort.

*[image 4: body map with inferior heart surface inset highlighted and epigastric band
shaded. Labels: "Inferior wall", "T7–T9", "Epigastrium"]*

4. Key point: inferior-wall MI
- Inferior/diaphragmatic MI sends pain via T7–T9 → felt in the epigastrium.
- Can be mistaken for indigestion; oesophagitis can also mimic MI.

🩺 Cardiac Pain (Acute Coronary Syndrome) — Treatment

*[image 1: simple flat flow, 4 blue boxes: "Chest pain + ECG" → "STEMI: reperfusion" /
"NSTE-ACS: risk score" → "Secondary prevention". No doses.]*

1. Treatment plan ➕ beyond source
- STEMI → open the artery fast (primary PCI preferred).
- NSTE-ACS → medicines + risk score decides timing of angiography.
- Everyone → long-term prevention.
💡 Exam tip: "time is muscle" — every minute of delay loses heart muscle.

*[image 2: 3D platelet close-up with blue drug molecules blocking surface receptors.
Labels: "Platelet", "Aspirin", "P2Y12 receptor", "No clot"]*

2. Main medicine: antiplatelets
- Which: aspirin + a P2Y12 inhibitor (e.g. ticagrelor).
- Why: stop platelets clumping on the ruptured plaque → fewer deaths and re-infarctions.
- Side effects: bleeding. Not for: active bleeding.

*[image 3: photorealistic cath lab, cardiologist guiding a catheter, screen showing a
coronary artery with a balloon and stent. Labels: "Catheter", "Balloon", "Stent"]*

3. Procedure: primary PCI
- A stent opens the blocked coronary artery.
- Chosen over clot-busting drugs when it can be done quickly; fibrinolysis if PCI is not
  available in time.

*[image 4: 4 friendly icon cards. Labels: "Stop smoking", "Statin daily", "Cardiac rehab",
"Chest pain? Call emergency"]*

4. Recovery & prevention
- Long-term: aspirin, statin, beta-blocker, ACE inhibitor (as indicated).
- Cardiac rehabilitation, quit smoking.
- New chest pain → call emergency services.
